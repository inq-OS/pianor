# ft_1_21: Challenges with characters

A character met in a scene of Keri challenges the student; the two take turns playing the phrases of the student's piece, and a standing of three steps decides it. It is the show of a character's learn, practise, show (d135), release R3. Decision: [d129](../decisions/game.md#d129). Form: call and response, as in the sword-insult duel but musical.

## Rules

A character's piece goes learn, practise, show ([d135](../decisions/game.md#d135)):

1. **Learn:** the student learns the piece from the character ([ft_1_22](ft_1_22-conversations-and-phrase-book.md)) or picks their own (d095), by reading and playing it on the staff in the room (AC21).
2. **Practise:** in the room and the trainers, until the evaluation ([core_12](core_12-evaluation-and-curriculum.md)) says it is good enough (AC19).
3. **Show:** the tug, played from memory with no staff (d100): level 1 checks only the keys and a wrong key moves on; level 2 is Tempo mode at the piece's tempo with the click; level 3 is Tempo mode with only the count-in heard. A loss can be retried after the cool-down of AC13.

Learning and practising pay no XP and take nothing away; no XP is ever taken away, and no screen says "failed". Every piece stays playable in free play.

- The character plays a phrase of the student's piece, the student plays the next from memory, and so on. Each student phrase is an exchange; winning exchanges pushes the standing toward the character, and at three steps it gives in.
- AX-0: it plays the student's own piece (d095), learned and practised before the show (d135); roles swap on each pass, so every bar is played. It is the third of learn, practise, show; the character's phrase is heard, never a falling-note hint (d100).
- AX-1: the show opens only on practice evidence (AC19); three steps and the shares (60, 80, 95 per cent) make a careless pass lose. A loss pays XP, and the retry comes after three clean plays (AC13): more practice, never punishment.
- AX-2.1: a character's lines are short and say what happened; no praise without a basis.
- Code: `src/core/exchange/tug.ts`, numbers in `content/challenge/rules.json`: `tugPart`, `tugPhrases` (AC3), `tugPlan` (AC4, AC6), `startTug`, `advanceTug`, `leaveTug` (AC4, AC5), `exchangeResult` (contract A.10), `tugPrize` (AC8; Kiur's practice tug pays nothing, AC14). The scene measures each student phrase's share (Wait: notes right first try; Tempo: `onTimeShare`) and passes it in. AX-3 provisional: level 1's half is rounded up, the lost prize rounded down.
- A finished tug is saved as a tug record in the profile (AC9).
- In the scene: the conversation's `tug` effect opens `src/game/adventure/tugView.ts` over `AdventureScene` on the character's phrase of that level (`CharacterDef.phrases`, d135). It plays the character's phrase with the piano (the figure in its talking state), listens to the student's with `WaitMode` (`moveOnWrong`, no staff), says the fact after each (`tugFact`, `tugFactLine`: notes right first try of the notes, and the first wrong key's reason, wrong octave, neighbour key or wrong letter; in Tempo mode with no wrong key, early or late, whichever more of its notes were; [d307](../decisions/game.md#d307)), and once the stall kill raised the share, the notes the phrase needed (`tugNeed`, "Needed 5 of 5"), and shows the standing on the painter's tug board (take B of sketch 1): the band gives way to a dark board with a rope between the student's token (left) and the character's (right, its face), a red rag that a won exchange pulls one place toward the student's end, chalk marks for every place, the token whose turn it is ringed in its player's colour with a note bobbing over it, and notes rising from the character while it plays (`core/layout/tug.ts`, `tugArt.ts`; AX-3 provisional sizes). The character's closing line is its content's `talk.<id>.tugWon` or `tugLost`. A finished tug goes to the world by `tugAfter` (`core/exchange/tugPlay.ts`: `recordTug`, the item by the `give` effect) and its record and XP to the profile by `tugRecord` and `ProfileService.completeTug` (AC9); Esc, or the corner Back (pressable over the tug, `overlayExit.ts`), leaves with nothing kept; in Wait mode, after 20 seconds of the student's turn with no note played, the character says once whose turn it is (`tug.yourTurn`, d307). The closing line ends with the XP the tug pays (`tugPrize`, none in a practice tug), and a line longer than a page ends each page but the last in a small arrow. Before it starts, `tugGate` (`core/exchange/tugGate.ts`) asks the practice gate of AC19 (the median score of the piece's last 10 room plays in the level's mode at least the level's share, at least 3 plays) and the cool-down of AC13 (after a lost tug of that piece, 3 clean plays since); while it is shut the character says what practice it waits for (`tug.wait.*`) and nothing is played. The prize per level is `prize` in `content/challenge/rules.json` (AX-3 provisional: 30, 45, 60 XP). Turn bars to chart bars: `turnChartBars`. The won item is the rung of the character's prize ladder for the level won (`CharacterDef.prizes`, `prizeFor`; AC18), an item of `content/items/items.json` (`core/item/item.ts`, the id, kind, name key and picture key of ft_1_20's item model); `AdventureScene` reads the world as the scene opens and shows its items in the band's inventory, named in a tooltip (AX-3 provisional: kind `kept`, which does nothing when pressed; only Keri's scenes show them); an item with a `picture` key shows its painted picture (`prizeArt.ts`: Puuvaim's oak leaf, acorn, golden leaf and seed of life), named in its tooltip, and one without shows its name: while any item has no picture the opened backpack is a list of whole names, one line each, as wide as the longest (two columns past seven), else a grid of pictures. A ladder stands in the band once, as its best rung held, with the rung in its tooltip (`TÕRU (2/5)`; `bandInventory` in `core/item/item.ts`); every rung stays in the world. The closing line names the prize (`{prize}` in `talk.<id>.tugWon`). Levels 2 and 3 (AC6) play in Tempo mode at the piece's tempo: a bar counted in, the character's phrase, and the student's next phrase on the beat after it on its own `Transport`, judged by `TempoMode`; level 2 clicks through both phrases, level 3 only counts in. A student phrase that comes first in its pass is played after the count-in alone. In Tempo mode the exchange holds the notes on time against the level's share, the number the fact says (`studentShare` by `onTimeShare`, `tugFact`). The level a talk opens is one above the best level won with the character, at least the talk's own (`tugLevelFor`; AX-3 provisional, no choice offered); its piece is the character's phrase of that level, and the practice gate asks for it in that level's mode. Tests: `tests/e2e/tug.spec.ts` (menu area; level 3 is not played there, only its click differs).

### What characters are (answered)

Three kinds: **spirits and nature that speak**, from old Estonian folk belief (the kratt and the like); **visitors** of the kinds that really come to Keri (a border guard dropping in for coffee, University of Tartu students checking instruments); **ghosts of the island's past** (the 19th-century warden).

- Folk belief is public domain; a modern book's own characters are not. *The Man Who Knew Snake Words* (Kivirähk, 2007) is under copyright: never its named characters, plot or pictures; only the folk motifs it draws on (snake words, forest people, a kratt).
- A visitor is a role, never a real person: no names, faces or likenesses of real guards, students or wardens. A ghost of a historical warden gets an invented name unless the maintainer gives one.

## Acceptance criteria

All AX-3 provisional (d129) until it has been playtested.

- **ft_1_21.AC1** Nothing the student sees or hears in a challenge, its results or the menus says battle, fight, monster, attack, health or damage; no label word names the contest either (Q6); the character's own line invites it, as in "No näita mulle, mida sa oskad!", and a character is called by its name, a generic name (an old man) or a generated one, all through string keys.
- **ft_1_21.AC2** A challenge opens from the character in the scene where it was met (ft_1_22.AC1), is drawn over that scene's picture, and returns there when it ends or is left. There is no battle stage.
- **ft_1_21.AC3** The part (level 1 the first half of the piece, levels 2 and 3 the whole piece, AC20) is cut into phrases of two bars, a pick-up bar joined to the first (d120); a last single bar is a phrase of its own. A pure core function gives the phrases and whose turn each is.
- **ft_1_21.AC4** The character plays the first phrase (the piece's sound, at the piece's tempo, the character animating while it plays) and the student the next, alternating to the end of the part; the judge listens only in the student's phrases. If the challenge is not decided at the end of the part, it starts over with the roles swapped.
- **ft_1_21.AC5** Each student phrase is one exchange: won when its share of notes right first try (in Tempo mode: on time, measured by `onTimeShare`) is at least the level's share (60, 80, 95 per cent), lost otherwise. A won exchange moves the standing one step toward the character, a lost one one step toward the student. The student wins at +3 and the character at −3 (`steps` in `content/challenge/rules.json`, planned).
- **ft_1_21.AC6** The levels are those of d100 on the student's phrases: no staff at any level; level 1 Wait mode where a wrong key moves on; level 2 Tempo mode at 100 per cent with the click, the student's phrase starting on the beat after the character's with no new count-in; level 3 Tempo mode with only the count-in heard.
- **ft_1_21.AC7** After each exchange the character says one line and one fact ("3 of 4 notes right", "came in late"), a wrong key keeping its reason (AX-0.2). The standing is shown at all times as one marker between the two (the look is the painter's).
- **ft_1_21.AC8** A won challenge pays the prize of its level (`prize` in `content/challenge/rules.json`). A lost one pays half the prize times the furthest standing reached toward the character, over 3, and at least 1 XP (a lost challenge still earns XP, AX-1.1; AX-3 provisional: the floor of 1, TODO(architect, R2): settle it with the other tug numbers), takes nothing away, and the character comes back for a new tug after the cool-down of AC13 (d132; the practice gate of AC19 is not asked again).
- **ft_1_21.AC9** A finished tug is saved in the profile as a tug record (its number, time, character, piece, won or lost, the XP it could pay, its exchanges and the XP paid), and its XP counts toward the student's total; a save from before tug records keeps every finished challenge as a tug record (profile document version 2, [core_11](core_11-save-documents.md)).
- **ft_1_21.AC10** The self-playing demo meets the first character on the knoll and plays a challenge to its end with the virtual player (`npm run demo`, a `challenge` scenario).
- **ft_1_21.AC12** (d132) A character can appear only once the student has practised enough in the room for it (its threshold in the character's content, counted from the practice records, never from time); then it turns up semi-randomly in an open scene: AX-3 provisional, on about every other day, and never more than three days in a row without it once it may come.
- **ft_1_21.AC13** (d132) After a lost tug the character stays away until the student has played that piece clean three more times in the room (from the practice records, never hours); the character says so when its tug is asked for (`tug.wait.clean`).
- **ft_1_21.AC14** (d132; changed since) The student can tug any learned piece with Kiur the trainer in the room, picked from the piece book: the same rules won and lost at 2 steps instead of 3, nothing won, lost or kept, and no cool-down.
- **ft_1_21.AC15** (d132) A won tug gives one item of that character into the band's inventory (ft_1_20); in the MVP it does nothing more than stand for the win, with the character's line naming it.
- **ft_1_21.AC16** (d132) A tug opens from a character's conversation ([ft_1_22](ft_1_22-conversations-and-phrase-book.md)), not on pressing the character; until the conversations are built it opens directly. At the advanced levels (more scenes open, R4) the exchanges become question and answer (the IDEA below).
- **ft_1_21.AC17** (if the player keeps tugging too long, win-lose-win-lose, the character eventually overpowers them) The stall kill: after 4 student phrases with no winner, each further phrase needs 5 percentage points more of its notes than the level's share (`stall.after`, `stall.raise`), never more than all of them, so a student who keeps winning and losing is overpowered and a phrase with every note right still wins. Kiur's tug too.
- **ft_1_21.AC18** (the prize a character gives is upgraded for every level of the character beaten, for example a tree spirit gives a normal leaf at level 1 and the seed of life at level 5) Each character has a prize ladder, one item per level: a won tug at level N gives rung N, each better than the one before, the last for the final level (the whole song, [ft_1_32](ft_1_32-learn-a-piece-bar-by-bar.md) slice 4). The band shows a ladder once, as the best rung held, in the place its first rung came, and names its rung and the ladder's length; every rung stays in the world (AX-1.1). The ladders are in [cast-and-phrases.md](../architect/cast-and-phrases.md) section 1. AX-3 provisional: five rungs per ladder (four song levels and the final boss, as a 64-bar song cuts); today's tug levels 1 to 3 give rungs 1 to 3, rungs 4 and 5 wait for the song levels. TODO(builder, R2): when the song levels come (ft_1_32 slice 4), the final level gives the ladder's last rung whatever the song's level count.
- **ft_1_21.AC19** AX-3 provisional (d135, AX-1): **practise, then show.** The show (the tug) opens for a level only when that piece's level in core_12 (the median of its last 10 room plays, d139), counted in the mode the level's show uses (Wait for level 1, Tempo for 2 and 3), reaches the level's share (60, 80, 95 per cent, the tug's level shares in `src/core/exchange/tug.ts`); under 3 plays it never opens. A pure core query answers it from the practice log; there is no timed screen in front of the tug. Until the gate opens, the character says in one line what practice it waits for (AX-2.1), never a number it made up.
- **ft_1_21.AC20** The player picks any piece of their list for a character's level (d095), or the piece the character taught (d135); level 1 plays its first half and levels 2 and 3 the whole piece, and the practice gate (AC19) and the show use the same piece.
- **ft_1_21.AC21** AX-3 provisional (d135): **learn.** A character's level names its piece: the one it teaches (ft_1_22.AC3, the whole piece or its phrases) or one the student picks from their list (AC20). Learning is reading and playing it on the staff in the room; nothing in the game plays it for the student. [human]
- **ft_1_21.AC22** The student understands learn, practise, show (what the character waits for, and why the tug opens), and losing stays gentle. [human]
- **ft_1_21.AC11** [human] A playtest of a challenge at each level with a student on the EZ-220; the maintainer says whether three steps and the shares are fair and whether level 1 from memory suits a young student.
