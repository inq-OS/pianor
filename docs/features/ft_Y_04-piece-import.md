# ft_Y_04: Piece import

Index: [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md).

Bring pieces in from files, MusicXML first, then MIDI. Slice 1 (built) reads MusicXML into a chart for analysis; slice 2 (built for MusicXML) turns that into a game piece; slice 3 (built) brings a photo of a sheet into the piece maker.

## Rules

- **Any length.** A whole piece of any length can be imported; splitting it into levels is the learning feature's job (ft_1_32).

**Slice 1, the reader (built: so that wished-for pieces can be measured and then added).**

- **Pure, no dependency** (`src/core/import/`): `xml.ts` a small XML reader (elements, attributes, text, CDATA, entities; prolog, comments and DOCTYPE skipped), `zip.ts` the zip directory of an `.mxl` with deflate given by the caller (Node's zlib in `tools/analysis/scoreFile.ts`, the browser's `DecompressionStream` in the analyser page), `musicxml.ts` score-partwise to a chart.
- **Everything that sounds keeps its time:** each staff of each part is a track (the upper staff of a piano part the right hand, the lower the left; a part of one staff by its clef); voices 1 to 4 per track in order of appearance; divisions to ticks with `backup` and `forward`; chords, ties, one grace note before a note, fingering, rests; dynamic marks `pp` to `ff` on the next note of their staff; slurs paired by part and number, kept when both ends are notes of one voice (the outer one of nested slurs); key, meter and tempo changes (a number, a metronome mark with dotted beats, else a tempo word from `TEMPO_WORDS`, AX-3 provisional, else 120); a pick-up measure (`implicit`, or a short first one) as long as its notes; plain repeats.
- **For analysis it simplifies and says so, never refuses:** clef changes, C clefs, a fifth voice, several grace notes, repeats with endings, microtones and times that are not whole ticks are kept as close as the chart allows, each listed once in `warnings` with how often. It refuses only what is not a readable score (not XML, score-timewise, no measures, no notes) or a reading that would break a chart rule, with the reason. Measured on music21's public-domain corpus (654 files): 653 read, the one refused is a drum part with no pitches.
- **Where it is used:** `readScore` in `tools/analysis/labelledSet.ts` (so CIPI's index trains, core_08.AC11), and the dev page `?demo=analyse` (`src/dev/analyseDemo.ts`): drop or choose files, see each one's difficulty, raw score, hardest bars, learned levels and what was simplified, easiest first, and open the difficulty panel on any passage. Files are read in the browser and never leave the computer.

**Slice 3, photo import (built: a PC utility, for the sheets of pieces students bring).**

- **A menu in the terminal** (`npm run piece:photo [folder]`, `tools/import/photo.ts`, wired in `photo-cli.ts` with Node's own readline, no dependency): the sheets in `private/photo/in/` (images, PDFs, or MusicXML that skips recognition), a title (the file name by default), then the report; read again, the next sheet, another folder, quit.
- **Recognition is Audiveris** (AGPL-3.0), a separate program the person installs and the menu starts (`-batch -export -output <dir> -- <image>`), found through `AUDIVERIS`, the installer's folders, then the PATH. It is never bundled, linked or copied into the repo. Its `.mxl` goes through the slice 1 reader.
- **The result is an own piece** (licence `NaN`, `private/photo/out/<id>.pianor-piece.json` and `<id>.report.txt`): school sheets are mostly copyrighted, so it never goes into `content/`. The person opens it with the piece maker's Import (ft_1_02b), fixes it against the paper and saves: that save is the human approval, as for any own piece.
- **What the piece maker cannot hold is listed, never dropped silently** (`fromChart`, `src/core/editor/fromChart.ts`, pure; the game importer of slice 2 writes ABC itself, because a game piece holds more than the piece maker: plain repeats written as signs): every note that fits comes in, dotted quarters, dotted eighths and sixteenths too (core_09.AC55); a length with no button (a triplet, a thirty-second, a double dot), a start off the sixteenth grid, a double sharp or flat, a second rhythm in one hand, a tie or grace whose partner was not taken, a bar holding more than its meter (a misread) are listed; a short first bar of whole beats whose last bar holds the rest of its bar opens as the piece maker's own pick-up (core_09.AC30); any other short first bar (a first bar the photo reading read short: refused for a sheet with no pick-up) becomes a whole bar with its notes at the end, as a pick-up is played, and is listed to check; and notes on a part for no hand are warned about, with bar, beat and hand for the person to fill by hand. Refused as a whole, with the reason: no notes, two parts for one hand, a meter other than 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, a meter or key change. Past the editor's 32 bars and tempo changes are warned about.
- **A photo is cleaned before Audiveris reads it** (`tools/import/clean.ts`, pure, JPEG and PNG through jpeg-js and fast-png; a PDF or TIFF goes as it is), because sheets from lessons are often phone photos with pencil marks: the light is evened (each pixel against the paper around it), only what is as dark as printed ink is kept (pencil is lighter: finger numbers, circles, slashes and handwriting drop out; a faint print with no black keeps everything darker than halfway to the paper), the page's turn is measured (up to 15 degrees), the staves are found and their outer lines followed to their ends, and the block of full-length staves is mapped back to a rectangle at a staff space of 24 pixels. Without it a test photo failed twice: no evenly spaced staff lines (taken at an angle), then the left hand lost (leaning bar lines kept the two staves of a system apart). The cleaned picture is kept next to the results (`<name>-<ext>-cleaned.png`) and the menu says Audiveris read it. When no staff is found, or the corners found cannot be a page (not four-sided, turned too far, an odd staff space, too big), Audiveris reads the photo as it is and the menu says why.
- **Not built:** the tablet (proposal: the doc "Photo import into the piece maker").

**Slice 4, the photo in the piece maker (built: the user sees what was imported and can add the original photo so the imported staff can be overlaid, without going back and forth with paper; the simplest way, so the user does not have to strain).**

- **Import takes a photo** (JPG or PNG) as well as a piece file, where the game's own server reads photos: the dev server and `start:local` on a PC with Audiveris (`tools/vite-photo.ts`, a Vite plugin; the browser cannot start a program). The server keeps the photo in `private/photo/in/`, reads it as `piece:photo` does (`readSheet`) and answers with the own piece file and the bars to fill by hand. The piece maker adds the piece, as any import, and opens it. The release zip has no such server: there Import takes piece files only, as before. With unsaved changes in the piece being written the piece maker asks for a save first, so opening the new piece loses nothing.
- **The photo lies behind the staff, bar by bar** (`photoPatches`, `src/core/editor/photoOverlay.ts`): the import writes a bar map beside the cleaned photo (`photo.json`: where the staves and the bar lines are, `photoLayout` in `clean.ts`), and each bar of the piece shows the same bar of the photo, its left and right bar lines fitted to the bar and its staff lines to the staff's lines, the ink in the cursor colour and half see-through, so a note the reading missed shows as a coloured head where it belongs. Fitted bar by bar because the piece maker spaces notes its own way, which one stretch of the whole photo could not follow. It is a page layer over the canvas that clicks pass through, hidden while a screen covers the piece maker.
- **Keys:** G shows or hides the photo; comma and full stop move it a bar back or on against the piece, for a bar the reading merged or split. The photo comes back whenever the piece is opened on that PC (the server finds it by the piece's id, also after an import numbered it).
- **Not built:** stretching the photo by hand (the cleaning straightens it); the photo on a tablet.

**Slice 5, the photo tool (built: put it all together into one interactive tool; an imported photo can be cropped to the relevant section, as sheets often carry examples and pieces that are not relevant).**

- **A plain page over the piece maker** (`src/game/editor/photoTool.ts`), a utility in the browser's own look, not the game's (a pure utility only). A photo chosen at Import opens it; the piece maker is paused behind it, so no key typed or played there writes anything. Esc or Close leaves the piece maker as it was.
- **Turn and crop:** Turn turns the photo a quarter turn (a photo taken on its side); a drag over the photo marks the part to read, the rest dimmed (`src/core/editor/photoCrop.ts`); Whole photo takes the mark away. The phone's own turn (EXIF) is applied by the browser, and the part sent is a JPEG of what is shown, so the server reads exactly the marked part. The name of the piece starts from the file name.
- **Read, then check:** Read sends the part to the server (slice 4); a failure goes back to the photo with the reason, to mark another part. What was read is shown before it opens: its name, bars and meter, the key signature and each hand's lowest and highest note, and the bars to fill by hand. The two things a photo reading most often gets wrong are put right here, against the sheet (`src/core/editor/photoFix.ts`): the key signature (a choice of the fifteen; the notes that took the read key take the chosen one, an accidental of its own stays) and a staff read in the wrong clef (one hand's notes moved twelve letters up or down, the bottom line of a treble staff being E4 and of a bass staff G2; never off the keyboard). Open the piece saves it as a new own piece and opens it with the photo behind the staff.
- **One id for both sides:** the piece maker saves a new own piece under the id the photo's folder has (`ownIdFor`, letters with marks as plain letters), so a title like "Väike täht" finds its photo.
- **Not built (AX-3 provisional, the simplest that met the ask):** reading again only the bars the first reading missed; placing a clef, key or corners by hand when the cleaning finds no staves (the photo then goes to Audiveris as it is, and the tool says why it could not read it). **Slice 2, the game importer (built for MusicXML: all the pieces the students want to play can be added).**

- **Command:** `npm run piece:import -- <score.musicxml|.mxl|.xml> [--id] [--title] [--out] [--license --source] [--rh <part>] [--lh <part>] [--force]` (`tools/import/piece.ts`, wired in `piece-cli.ts`). It reads the score (slice 1), writes ABC (`chartToAbc`, `src/core/import/toAbc.ts`, pure), compiles and lints it exactly as the game loads it, checks the staff can draw it, and only then writes `piece.abc` and `piece.meta.json` into `<out>/<id>/`. The id comes from the title (lowercase words and dashes, accents dropped). It says the bars, notes, hands, level, the estimator's difficulty and every warning.
- **Two folders:** the default is the gitignored `private/pieces/`: the pieces students wish for are mostly copyrighted, so they stay on this machine with licence `NaN` (like an own piece, ft_1_02b) and `unlockLevel: 1`. The game plays them (`tools/vite-pieces.ts`): checked like own pieces, without the level limits on chords, accidentals and keys, kept whenever they compile, their problems reported and never failing a build; one with the id of a `content/pieces` piece is left out. The end-to-end tests' server leaves the folder out (`PIANOR_NO_PRIVATE_PIECES`). `--out content/pieces` is for scores with an open licence (`--license`, one the linter knows, and `--source`, both typed by the person, never guessed), and only when the piece passes the linter at a level, so `piece:lint` and the CI stay green.
- **A draft until a person approves it:** `status: "draft"` (the dev server shows it; `npm run start` and `start:local` do not). A person who has played it through sets `"status": "approved"`; agents never do. The meta has `generator: { kind: "import", params: { format: "musicxml", file } }`, `tags: ["imported"]`, no phrases, no styles, and the lowest level it fits (else the highest), with no `difficulty`: a person confirms it.
- **Everything the game's ABC subset holds comes over note for note** (au02): lengths from whole to sixteenth with dotted halves, quarters and eighths; chords; 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4; any key; accidentals to the bar line, double ones, and one carried by a tie; fingerings, dynamics, ties, slurs, one grace note, plain repeats; a pick-up (a last bar with nothing past what the pick-up lacks is cut to it, a short one filled with rests, and when the last bar is full of notes the pick-up becomes a whole bar with rests in front, warned). Rests are written into every gap; voices are written four bars to a line. The voices of one hand are read as one line of chords: two voices that move together become that hand's chords, notes in unison are played once (warned).
- **The notes are never changed in silence.** What the notes themselves need and the subset lacks refuses the whole piece and writes nothing, every place listed with bar (as printed, a pick-up is 0), beat and hand: a triplet or other tuplet, a thirty-second, a dotted sixteenth, a double dot, a note off the sixteenth grid, two voices that move apart in one hand, a note across a bar line. Refused as a whole with the reason: a meter other than 4/4, 3/4, 2/4, a meter or key change, a bar longer than its meter or a short bar inside the piece, more than one part for a hand (`--rh`, `--lh` choose; the others are left out, warned). A mark the subset cannot hold where it stands is left out with a warning that says where: a tie on some notes of a chord, a tie with no target, a tie or slur across a repeat sign, a slur over another or over one pitch, a grace before a chord, on the second note of a tie, of its main note's pitch, or a second one; a second dynamic on one chord (the first is kept). Tempo changes keep the first tempo (warned).
- **Measured** on music21's public-domain corpus: of 654 scores, 12 are written and all 12 compile; most of the rest are chorales of four parts (more than one part for a hand) or have meter changes or 6/8. The pieces students wish for are piano scores, which fare better; to be measured on their files.
- **Not built:** MIDI (no spelling, hands or clean rhythm, so the hand split and quantising are guesses to review); importing in the browser; phrases proposed from double bar lines and rehearsal marks; the preview page listing the private folder (approval is the meta file's `status` for now); repeats with endings, 6/8 and other meters, meter and key changes, tuplets, which wait on the compiler and the staff (core_09).
- TODO(builder, R2): write a triplet of eighths or quarters that starts on a beat as `(3` (the compiler and the staff take them since core_09.AC49 and AC50, d163) instead of refusing the piece; other tuplets stay refused.
- **Copyright:** only pieces written for the project or with an open licence go into `content/pieces/`; everything else stays in `private/pieces/`, never committed or deployed (the game runs on this machine only, d068).

## Acceptance criteria

- **ft_Y_04.AC1** The XML reader reads elements, attributes in either quote, text, CDATA and character entities, skips the prolog, comments, processing instructions and a DOCTYPE with an internal subset, and says on which line a broken document breaks (`src/core/import/xml.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC2** The zip reader lists an archive's stored and deflated entries and reads them with a given deflate, and refuses a damaged or unknown archive with the reason (`src/core/import/zip.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC3** A MusicXML score reads to a valid chart whose notes, fingers, bars, tempo and difficulty equal those of the same piece written in ABC (`tests/fixtures/musicxml/hands-together.musicxml` against `tests/fixtures/pieces/hands-together`); chords, voices, pick-ups, triplets, graces, dynamics, slurs, tempo marks, meter and key changes and repeats read as in the rules; what was simplified is listed, and what is not a score is refused with why (`src/core/import/musicxml.test.ts`, `tools/analysis/scoreFile.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC4** A compressed `.mxl` reads to the same chart as its plain file, through the rootfile `META-INF/container.xml` names (`tools/analysis/scoreFile.test.ts`, `src/core/import/musicxml.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC5** The analyser page scores chosen MusicXML and `.mxl` files in the browser, easiest first, lists a file it cannot read with the reason, and opens the difficulty panel for a file, any passage included (`tests/e2e/analyse.spec.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC6** A chart whose notation the piece maker holds becomes an editor piece note for note: the editor's own chart of it has the same notes, spellings, fingers, meter, key and tempo, with chords, ties, slurs, dynamics, graces and repeats kept, all six as the MusicXML reader gives them (`src/core/editor/fromChart.test.ts`, the fixture in `tools/import/photo.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC7** What the piece maker cannot hold is listed with bar, beat and hand and the other notes kept; what cannot be a piece maker piece at all is refused with the reason (`src/core/editor/fromChart.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC8** The photo import menu lists the sheets of its folder, reads an image through Audiveris (a MusicXML file without it) and writes an own piece file the piece maker imports unchanged (licence `NaN`) and a report of the places to fill by hand; a failed recognition or a score that is not taken is said with its reason and writes nothing (`tools/import/photo.test.ts`). Real recognition on a Windows machine: needs human.
- **ft_Y_04.AC15** A photo of a sheet taken at an angle or turned up to 15 degrees, on uneven light, with pencil marks, broken staff lines or a faint print comes out level, its staves evenly spaced at a staff space of 24 pixels, their bar lines upright, the pencil gone; a picture with no staff, or corners that cannot be a page, is not replaced, with the reason (`tools/import/clean.test.ts`, synthetic pictures; photos of real sheets are never committed). On a real sheet it took Audiveris from no music to both hands, 53 notes and two bars to fill by hand, in a cloud session: on a Windows machine, needs human.
- **ft_Y_04.AC9** A chart written as a game piece and compiled again plays the same notes with the same spellings, fingers, ties, dynamics, slurs, graces, repeats, bars, meter, key and tempo: every length from whole to sixteenth, dotted ones, rests, chords in both hands, accidentals held, double and carried by a tie, a pick-up (cut, filled, or made whole); two voices of one hand that move together become chords (`src/core/import/toAbc.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC10** What the notes need and the subset lacks refuses the piece with every place by bar (as printed), beat and hand; a meter, key or bar the game cannot write, or two parts for a hand, refuses it with the reason (`src/core/import/toAbc.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC11** A tie, slur, grace note or second dynamic on one chord that the subset cannot hold where it stands is left out with a warning that names its bar, beat and hand, and the rest compiles (`src/core/import/toAbc.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC12** `piece:import` writes a draft piece folder that compiles to the score's notes: into `private/pieces/` by default with licence `NaN` and `unlockLevel: 1`, whatever levels it fits; into `content/pieces/` only with an open licence, a source and a level it passes (`tools/import/piece.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC13** `piece:import` writes nothing and says why for a score it cannot read or write (each place listed), a folder or id already taken, or an id that is not lowercase words and dashes (`tools/import/piece.test.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC16** After a photo import the piece maker shows each bar of the piece over the same bar of the photo, staff lines on staff lines, from the bar map the import wrote (the bars a stem crosses are no bars); a shift moves the photo a bar against the piece, only the page shown is drawn, a photo of one staff goes on its hand's staff (`src/core/editor/photoOverlay.test.ts`, `tools/import/clean.test.ts`, `tools/import/photo.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/editor.spec.ts`).
- **ft_Y_04.AC17** Where the game's server reads photos, Import takes a photo, which the server keeps and reads into the own piece the piece maker then opens, naming the bars to fill by hand; the server refuses what is not a photo, an empty upload and a second photo at once, says why reading failed, and serves a piece's photo only from inside its folder (`tools/vite-photo.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/editor.spec.ts` with the server's answers given by the test). The whole way with Audiveris on a real sheet's photo ran in a cloud session; on a Windows machine: needs human.
- **ft_Y_04.AC18** The photo tool turns the photo by quarter turns, marks a part by a drag (kept inside the photo, a click marks nothing) and sends only that part; it shows what was read with each hand's range and the bars to fill, puts a missed key signature right (accidentals of their own kept) and moves a hand read in the wrong clef by twelve letters (never off the keyboard); Esc closes it with the piece maker as it was and no note written (`src/core/editor/photoCrop.test.ts`, `src/core/editor/photoFix.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/editor.spec.ts` with the server's answers given by the test). A real sheet's photo was cropped, read with Audiveris and opened with its photo behind the staff in a cloud session; on a Windows machine: needs human.
- **ft_Y_04.AC14** The game gets the private pieces that compile, checked without the level limits on chords, accidentals and keys, a draft only on the dev server, and never one whose id a `content/pieces` piece has (`tools/vite-pieces.test.ts`). Playing an imported wish piece on the EZ-220: needs human.
