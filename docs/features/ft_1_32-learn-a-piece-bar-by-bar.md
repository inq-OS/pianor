# ft_1_32: Learn a piece bar by bar

The design (concept doc "Learning a piece: baseline tempo and practice loop"). Index: [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md).

AX-0: the student reads every bar they play and never rehearses a mistake. AX-1: perfection is required, made winnable by a small unit that grows. AX-2: the reward is a note value the student learns from (four quarters make a whole note), not praise.

## Rules

1. **Units.** A piece is learned in printed bars; a pick-up belongs to the first full bar. A piece of one bar is one unit.
2. **Chunks and steps.** Units are cut into chunks of 4 (the last chunk may be shorter). Inside a chunk the run grows by the step size (1 bar, or 2 for a player set to 2): bars 1, 1-2, 1-3, 1-4. A step that adds only bars with nothing to play is skipped. The finished chunk is then played once more, the **confirmation play**. After every second chunk the two are joined (bars 1-8), and joins double while they are shorter than the piece. Last comes the whole piece, three perfect runs in a row (AX-1: more correct repetitions for the same clef; a miss takes nothing away and the run starts over); a piece of one chunk ends on its confirmation play, which is the whole piece and so is asked three times in a row as well. So every step is confirmed by a final test that puts it together, a bar, a chunk, a pair of chunks at a time. Confirmation plays, joins and the whole run are measured (rule 8).
   What is learned is short: a character teaches a **piece of a song**, 4 bars (one chunk) or 8 (two) for now, up to about 16, as a level of that character; a 64-bar song is four levels of 16 bars, and the character's final boss is all its bars in one sitting, in the tug (slice 4).
3. **A perfect run.** Wait mode with the staff showing only the step's bars. Every note is right the first time. The first wrong key ends the run at once, Kiur (or the character teaching it) names the mistake, and the same step starts again; no new bar appears until it is perfect. Note names may be shown (for students who do not read the staff yet), but a run with them does not count: the next run hides them. Finger numbers always count.
4. **Progress** is kept per player and piece. On a later day than the last run, the bars of the step before the current one are played once, perfectly, before the next bar appears (the recall check, AX-1). The check is at most one chunk: after a join it is the last chunk joined, never the join's 8 or 16 bars (the recall rule is the builder's; AX-3 provisional).
5. **Rewards.** Each secured bar is a quarter note in a row under the staff; a finished chunk's quarters merge into one note of the chunk's length (four bars a whole note, two a half note, three a dotted half); the learned piece gets a treble clef by its name. Each has a medal colour for how cleanly it was taken in: a step finished with no run that did not count is gold, one or two silver, more bronze, and a measured run asked again for its uneven pace is such a run too (decided from the axioms; AX-1: gold is clean and even at the first try); a chunk takes the lowest of its bars, the piece the lowest of its chunks, confirmation plays, joins and whole run. Three perfect plays in a row of a bar (the practice log) raise it to gold, and with it its chunk and piece: the player is never locked into a first result. XP is counted as before, never shown as the reward here.
6. **Mistake markers** (an option per player, on by default). A small circle above a note that was missed at least twice (setting, default 2) in its bar's last 10 plays, from the practice log's misses; the switch and the threshold are the player's learning settings (`settings.learn`, an optional save field). Light red at the threshold, full red two misses later (the 4th miss by default, the 3rd with the threshold at 1): every miss is a mistake practised. A note played right again cools down.
7. `AX-3 provisional:` chunk 4, step 1, recall on a new calendar day, medal limits and the marker window of 10 are constants in `src/core/learn/`; the rushing stop is rule 12.
8. **The tempo is measured, never asked** (the confirmation play establishes baseline data). A learning run is Wait mode, where every right key has its time: the gaps between notes against their written lengths give the pace the student chose (BPM), how evenly it was kept, and where it stopped (`src/core/learn/tempo.ts`). A confirmation play, a join and the whole run count only when played evenly (no stop of three times a note's length, three gaps in five within 50% of the run's own pace, so a beginner's wobble counts, [d281](../decisions/play.md#d281)); an uneven one loses nothing and Kiur asks it again, as evenly as the student can, at most once for a step: the next run with every note right then counts with no pace kept, so the pace never holds a student at a step. An uneven run of the whole piece is not saved as a pass. The paces of the counted runs are kept with the progress; their median, rounded down to 5, is the piece's baseline tempo, which slice 3 gives to Tempo mode. No metronome, and the student is never asked to keep a given speed; Kiur says the pace that was played.
9. **What the measured runs showed is kept** (the deviation or standard deviation is included). Every measured run with all notes right, even or not, leaves one record with the progress (`LearnProgress.tests`, the latest 60): its day, step and kind of test (a chunk's confirmation, a join, the whole piece), the pace, the **standard deviation** of its gaps around its own pace in percent (a stop counts in full), the same as a **robust spread** that a stop does not move (median absolute deviation times 1.4826), the **drift** of the tempo from the run's start to its end (the Theil-Sen trend of the gaps: above 0 the student rushed), the stops, whether it was even, the **pace of each bar** against the run's own (from the gaps that begin in the bar), and, when both hands play, **each hand's own robust spread**. True numbers, no grade.
10. **The stats are pictures, and can be filtered** (always shown visually and filterable). A piece with such records has a third button on its stats page (`S`) that opens the learning stats screen (`LearnStatsScene`, rules in `src/core/learn/stats.ts`). A row of buttons chooses the measure (tempo, spread, wobble, drift, stops, hands); two buttons step through which tests (all, chunks, joins, whole) and which days (all, the last seven, today). The graph shows the chosen measure over the runs, oldest left: a dot a run (red for an uneven one), for the tempo a whisker of one standard deviation and the piece's baseline as a dotted line, for the hands the left hand as rings; the Theil-Sen trend as a line once four runs have a value, and in words beside the filters the latest value and the change per test. Under it the piece's bars in a strip, each tinted by its middle pace over the runs shown (four tints, the darkest at 160% of the run's pace or slower; grey for a bar not measured).
11. **A page says what the numbers mean and what to work on.** The `?` on that screen opens pages in words. The first says what to work on, from the latest three runs, the most telling first and at most three: stops (with the slowest bar), a slow bar, a tempo that rises or falls through a run, the hand that wobbles more, uneven playing, a tempo that differs from test to test; when none shows, that the playing is even and at what tempo. The pages after it say what each measure, the bar strip and the trend line mean, and what to aim for. AX-2.1: every sentence is a measured fact and one thing to do, never a grade.
12. **The rushing stop.** Once a piece has a baseline tempo, a step run that speeds up beyond it is stopped at once and asked again; Kiur says so ("ÄRA KIIRUSTA, PROOVIME UUESTI!"). Nothing was played wrong, so nothing is lost: it counts as a run asked again, like an uneven measured run, and after `TEMPO_RULES.asks` of those the step's runs count at any speed, so a student who keeps rushing is never held at a step. The recall check is never stopped for speed. `AX-3 provisional:` rushing is the median pace of the latest 3 gaps more than 20% above the baseline (`RUSH_RULES` in `src/core/learn/tempo.ts`).
13. **Hands apart, if chosen** ([d284](../decisions/game.md#d284)). A two-hand piece is learned with both hands together by default. A player who chooses hands apart in the learning settings (`settings.learn.handsApart`, an optional field) learns a new two-hand piece with each growing step played by the right hand alone, then the left hand alone, then both; a hand step waits for that hand only (`handChart`) and shows both staves, the resting hand's notes grey (`restingTracks`, AC18), and a step whose bars have only one hand's notes is played once. Confirmation plays, joins and the whole run are always both hands. A piece keeps the choice its learning began with (`LearnProgress.handsApart`, optional, d069.3), as it keeps its step size.
14. **The piano is a sandbox** ([d278](../decisions/game.md#d278)). A piece picked at the piano plays in full with no learning and no limits, except a character's piece, which is learned bar by bar first as before. Learning goes through Kiur: pressed in the room he asks Learn or Tug as two pictures (an open music book, a rope with its red rag; their names only in their bubbles, `learnBook` and `tugRope` in `standArt.ts`), and Learn opens the piece book; the piece picked opens in learning even when it was played to the end before (`learnsOnOpen` in `src/core/learn/route.ts`).
15. **A tug needs a learned piece.** Kiur's Tug opens the piece book listing only the pieces whose whole plan is done (`pieceLearned`), with a line to learn one first when there are none; the room tugs only such a piece, and for any other Kiur says to learn it with him first and nothing is played. Free plays never count. With learning off (`?learn=0`) every piece may be tugged.
16. **A character teaches by learning** (slice 4). A character's "teach me" opens its piece in learning bar by bar, as Kiur's Learn does; Back, the door, or the piece learned goes back to the character's scene. Every piece of a met character's `phrases` that is learned is kept as taught with that character, on that day (`learnedTaught` in `src/core/exchange/teach.ts`), however it was learned. A character's tug waits for its piece to be learned before any other practice wait (`tugGate`'s `learn` wait, `tug.wait.learn`). With learning off (`?learn=0`, the self-playing demo) the three tries of ft_1_22.AC3 stay and any piece may be tugged.
17. **Go on or start over.** A piece left past its first step and opened again in learning (not a restart between runs) pauses with two pictures and no words: go on where the learning stopped (also Esc), or start over from the first step (`LearnResumeScene`, d285). Starting over clears the steps and medals and plans the piece with the player's settings now, its hands and step size; the measured paces and runs stay (`startOver`, `halfLearned` in `src/core/learn/progress.ts`).
18. **The run measured while it is played.** With the stats pane on, a learning run shows at the pane's left edge its tempo, spread and wobble so far (`LearnSession.liveTempo`, the measures of rule 9 named as on the learning stats screen), a dash before three notes are played; it updates on every note and decides nothing.

## Acceptance criteria

- **ft_1_32.AC1** The step plan of a piece: growing steps inside chunks of 4 units, a confirmation play of each finished chunk, joins that double, the whole piece last, three perfect runs in a row (a piece of one chunk ends on its confirmation play, asked as often); a pick-up joins the first bar; steps that add only empty bars are skipped; step size 1 or 2 (slice 1).
- **ft_1_32.AC2** Progress: a perfect run moves to the next step, a stopped run repeats the step, and a stopped or note-name run breaks a run of whole-piece plays; the piece is learned after its last step (a step asked several times needs them in a row); on a later day the step before is played once first (slice 1).
- **ft_1_32.AC3** A run is perfect when every note was right the first time with no note names shown; the first wrong key ends it; a run with note names counts nothing and hides them for the next run (slice 1).
- **ft_1_32.AC4** Rewards: a quarter note per secured bar, merged into one note per finished chunk, a clef for the learned piece, each with a gold, silver or bronze medal from the runs that did not count, raised to gold by three perfect plays in a row (slice 1 core, slice 2 screen).
- **ft_1_32.AC5** Mistake markers: from the practice log, at the threshold's miss in the bar's last 10 plays, full red two misses later, cooling down when played right (slice 1 core, slice 2 screen).
- **ft_1_32.AC6** The practice screen shows only the step's bars, stops the run on the first wrong key with Kiur's line, and keeps progress across days (slice 2).
- **ft_1_32.AC7** A confirmation play, a join and the whole run are measured from their key times: the pace, its evenness and its stops; an uneven run is asked again and loses nothing; the counted paces are kept, and their median rounded down to 5 is the baseline tempo (slice 2).
- **ft_1_32.AC8** Every measured run with all notes right keeps its pace, the standard deviation of its gaps, the robust spread, the drift, its stops and whether it was even, with the progress; a record never fails the save of the progress (slice 2).
- **ft_1_32.AC9** A record also keeps the kind of test, the pace of each bar and each hand's spread; the learning stats screen shows every measure as a graph over the runs with its trend, filtered by kind of test and by days, and the piece's bars tinted by their pace (slice 2).
- **ft_1_32.AC10** The screen's `?` opens pages that say what to work on, from the latest runs, and what each number means and what to aim for (slice 2).
- **ft_1_32.AC11** With a baseline tempo, a step run whose latest gaps are faster than the baseline by more than the margin is stopped at once and asked again with Kiur's line; it loses nothing and counts as a run asked again, at most `TEMPO_RULES.asks` times a step; never without a baseline or in the recall check (slice 3).
- **ft_1_32.AC12** A two-hand piece is learned with both hands together unless the player chose hands apart in the learning settings; with hands apart each growing step of a new piece comes three times, the right hand alone, the left hand alone, then both, a hand step judging that hand only; a step with one hand's notes only comes once; a piece begun keeps the choice it began with (rule 13).
- **ft_1_32.AC13** A piece picked at the piano plays in full with no learning unless it is a character's piece; a piece picked through Kiur's Learn opens in learning, even after full plays (rule 14).
- **ft_1_32.AC14** Kiur's Tug lists only learned pieces and opens the tug only for one; for any other he says to learn it first and nothing is played (rule 15).
- **ft_1_32.AC15** A character's "teach me" opens its piece in learning bar by bar and leaving goes back to its scene; a learned piece of a met character is kept as taught; a character's tug waits for its piece to be learned (rule 16).
- **ft_1_32.AC16** A piece left halfway through learning asks, when opened again, with two pictures whether to go on or start over; starting over begins at the first step with the settings now and keeps the measured runs (rule 17).
- **ft_1_32.AC17** With the stats pane on, a learning run shows its tempo, spread and wobble so far, from three notes played, updated on every note (rule 18).
- **ft_1_32.AC18** A hand step shows both staves, the resting hand's notes grey and never awaited (rule 13).

## Slice 2 as built

- A piece opens in learning when it is a character's piece picked at the piano or a piece picked through Kiur's Learn (rule 14), and the player has not learned it yet (`src/game/practice/LearnSession.ts`): not in a draft preview, the teacher's check-up or a section, and not with `?learn=0` (the end-to-end tests) or under autoplay. `AX-3 provisional:` at the piano, a piece played to the end three times before its learning began counts as learned; through Kiur it is learned all the same.
- The run is Wait mode; the staff shows only its bars, drawn at the size of the whole piece. The mode, tempo and section buttons are off, as in the check-up; Listen and Start again stay. Note names stay off for the run after one with names (Kiur says so when the switch is pressed).
- The first wrong key stops the judge; the wrong key stays on the staff, Kiur names the mistake and its bar, the bar is logged with its miss (`BarRecorder.stop`), and the step starts again. A perfect run says so and opens the next step; a measured run says the pace it was played at, and one of the whole piece is also saved as a pass (XP as before).
- The reward notes are a row in the tools strip, or with the band at the right end of Kiur's strip (`rewardNotes.ts`); the mistake markers are rings over the notes (`StaffView.setMarkers`), on every practice screen of a piece while the player's setting is on.
- Progress (with the measured paces, `bpm`, and the measured runs, `tests`) and settings are optional save fields (`learning`, `settings.learn`; d069.3, no version bump).
- A piece keeps the step size its learning began with (`LearnProgress.stepBars`, `learnStepBars`): a changed setting applies to the next piece, so the saved step index and medals never point at other steps. The moment a piece is learned is kept (`learnedAt`), and only plays after it raise a bar to gold (`goldenBars`); the learning runs themselves never do. Both are optional save fields (d069.3).
- The learning settings are the second page of the settings screen (the Learning action in the band, lit while the page shows): the bars a step adds (1 or 2), the rings over missed notes on or off, the miss they come at (1 to 5), and two-hand pieces with both hands together or hands apart (rule 13). `AX-3 provisional:` a second page, since the first one's four rows are full with the band.

## Numbers kept by the architect

Weighed against the axioms; they stay as built until real runs of the student say otherwise:
- `MAX_LEARN_TESTS` 60 runs kept per piece: storage only, enough for a trend over weeks; "even" stays the share of gaps within 50%, not the robust spread.
- `FOCUS_RULES` (the latest 3 runs, a bar slow from 130%, drift from 10%, uneven from 15%, a hand 8 points worse, tempo differing by 15%), the trend from 4 runs, the strip's full tint at 160%: they only choose which measured fact to say first (AX-2.1), so no choice among them changes what the student plays.
- `TEMPO_RULES` (a gap even within 50% of the run's pace, a stop at three times a note's length, three gaps in five even and no stop, an uneven run asked again at most once, d281): set so a beginner reading in Wait mode is not held back for the pace; a tighter rule would add repetitions (AX-1) but cannot be checked against the calibration line without real runs.
- `KNOWN_COMPLETIONS` 3: a piece played to the end three times before its learning began counts as learned. That student has read every bar three times; sending them back to bar 1 would trade practice of their hard spots (sections, Tempo mode) for bars they already play (AX-0).
