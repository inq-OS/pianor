# core_08: Difficulty estimator

How hard a piece, a bar or a phrase is. One scale, 0 to 100 points in five grades, Beginner to Master ([d157](../decisions/game.md#d157)); its Beginner grade is the ten steps of [d138](../decisions/game.md#d138) (anchors in [cast-and-phrases.md](../architect/cast-and-phrases.md) section 2): the estimator proposes, a person confirms by writing `difficulty` in the meta file, in half stars. AX-0: it must reward harder music, never the easy way. Index: [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md).

## Rules

1. **Two numbers, one model.** A *prior* from the notes alone (slice A, built) and a *measured* one from how players actually do (slice B). Both are per bar; a piece is the mean of its bars weighted by their presses, plus half the gap to the hardest bar, so one hard spot counts.
2. **Prior** (`difficultyOf` in `src/core/piece/difficulty.ts`, pure). Per bar, over the play tracks only: onsets per second at the written tempo (tempo changes inside a bar included); rhythm, the different written lengths of notes and rests weighted by kind (plain 1, dotted 1.5, anything else a tuplet 2.5) minus one; leap points, diatonic steps beyond a step per melodic line, the jump into the bar included, a chord being no jump; five-finger shifts per hand (the notes since the last shift may span at most 4 diatonic steps); accidentals the key signature does not give; extra chord notes; the share of presses made while the other hand sounds; voices in one hand beyond the first; grace notes. A tied-on note is no press, a grace note is. A bar with nothing to play scores 0 and is left out of the mean. The piece adds meter, key and tempo changes, the first key signature's sharps or flats, the range beyond seven semitones, both clefs read, and the length (per doubling past 4 bars). Cut-offs turn the score into the step 1 to 10. Every input is ticks and spelled pitches, so dotted notes, triplets, two voices in one hand and imported pieces (core_09, ft_Y_04) are scored the day the compiler makes them.
3. **One scale, two units (d157).** `points` 1 to 100 in five grades of 20: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Expert, Master; a grade's five stars are 4 points each and fill partly (a quarter star per point; `complexityOf`, `src/core/properties/complexity.ts`), and a grade's top is its own (20 is Beginner with five stars, 21 Intermediate with a quarter star). A person confirms `difficulty` in half stars, 1 to 50, two points each (`scale`, `confirmedPoints`): 1 to 10 is the Beginner grade, exactly the steps of d138, so phrases, tug levels and every confirmed number keep their meaning (published graders use about nine levels, Henle's 1 to 9, and the research of Ramoneda et al. still guesses only about 40 % of those exactly, so a person checks half stars, not points). Below `beginnerEnd` (score 21) step N is the points 2N-1 and 2N; above it the points climb towards 100 (`tail`), AX-3 provisional until fitted to Henle levels (rule 12, CIPI): the rules score orders concert pieces poorly (music21's corpus: Bach's C major prelude scores above Mozart's K545 first movement), so nothing above Beginner is a fact yet.
4. **Any passage** (`difficultyOfPassage`): written bar ranges played one after another, in any order (a part, a tug turn, a loop section, part A straight into part C), scored with the jumps and hand shifts across each seam and the bars kept at their written numbers; `partDifficulties` gives each named part its own. The piece preview lists each part's points.
5. **Why, by dimension.** Every bar's and piece's score splits into eight dimensions that add up to it: tempo (onsets per second), rhythm, pitch (leaps), hands (shifts, range), reading (accidentals, key signature, both clefs), texture (chords, hands together, voices, graces), structure (meter, key and tempo changes) and length. They are the axes the player profile will share.
6. **The difficulty panel** in the piece preview (`src/dev/difficultyPanel.ts`): the whole piece's line and its dimensions, each part's points, the bars as a strip tinted by heat (the bar's score against the hardest bar, the practice analysis' colours), and a passage chosen by clicking a bar, shift-clicking to extend, or typing printed bars in any order (`1-4, 9-12`, `parseBarList`). The passage shows its line, its dimensions and one line of features per bar; the staff shows the heat and numbers the passage's ranges in play order.
7. **Hardest bars:** at most three bars at 1.25 times the piece's mean or more, numbered as printed (a pick-up is 0, d120).
8. **Weights.** `DIFFICULTY_WEIGHTS`, beside the code. AX-3 provisional: a first guess that puts Puuvaim's phrases on steps 1, 2, 3 and the first set of pieces between 1 and 6; they move to a content file when the calibration script (rule 11) fits them, since nobody tunes them by hand before that.
9. **Where it shows.** `piece:lint` prints each piece's proposal, score and hardest bars, and warns (`difficulty`) when a confirmed number is 2 or more half stars away. The piece preview shows the points in its list (`12p`) and the grade and stars in its facts line. The info card shows the grade's name and its five stars, filled partly, from the confirmed number, else the proposal (`complexityOf`, core_05). The student never sees the number itself.
10. **Measured** (slice B, built: `measuredDifficulty` in `src/core/progression/measuredDifficulty.ts`, pure). The practice log (core_10) holds per-bar plays: a bar's share is its notes right the first time over its notes, in the player's latest 10 plays of that bar (whole piece and sections alike, bars with nothing to play left out); its measured difficulty is the gap from the piece's mean share to its own, above 0 harder for this player. A bar with fewer than 3 plays keeps its prior and no gap; a piece with fewer than two bars measured is not measured and keeps the prior's hardest bars. The hardest bars for the player are at most three with a gap of 0.1 or more, in bar order. AX-3 provisional: 10 plays, 3, 0.1 and three (`MEASURED_RULES`). No save-format change.
11. **Calibration** (slice B, built). `npx tsx tools/analysis/calibrate-cli.ts <export.json>...` reads players' exported practice logs (export version 2), takes each measured bar's gap (rule 10) and its features minus the piece's mean, fits the bar weights by ridge least squares (never in the game, at least 30 measured bars), sets a weight the fit puts below 0 to 0, rescales so the summed bar score is unchanged, and prints today's and the suggested weights side by side with how well each ranks the hard bars. It writes nothing: a person accepts the weights by writing them into `DIFFICULTY_WEIGHTS.bar` and running `npm test`, which holds the curriculum order and the confirmed numbers.
12. **Learned** (`src/core/piece/learnedModel.ts`, `tools/analysis/`). A ridge model over the prior's features (the eight dimensions, the bars' log length, mean, peak and hard share, the range and the key signature) fitted to any graded set read through one neutral manifest (`labelledSet.ts`: name, licence, `publishable`, the label's scale, items with a composer group, a label and score files). `npx tsx tools/analysis/train-cli.ts <manifest.json>`, or `--cipi <cipi/index.json>` for CIPI (Henle 1 to 9, `cipi.ts`), checks it by cross-validation with whole composers held out (mean error, within one step, exact, rank correlation), fits on every item and writes the model with those numbers. A work in movements takes, feature by feature, its hardest movement (AX-3 provisional). The piece preview's panel shows each model's level for the piece and the passage, read through the dev server only.
13. **Private until a person says otherwise.** A set's `publishable` is set only by a person who read its licence. A model on a set that is not publishable (CIPI is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, research only) goes to the gitignored `private/models/`, is never committed or built into the game, and is marked private wherever it shows; a publishable one goes to a `models` folder in `content/`. The CIPI files themselves are requested from Zenodo by the maintainer and never committed.
14. **Use for reward** (slice C). Piece XP times a factor from the prior (for example 0.75 to 1.5), known before the first play and the same for every student. The measured number only chooses: which bars the section loop and the part cards point at, which phrases a character offers first. It never lowers XP already earned (AX-1.1).
15. **Fitting the grades above Beginner** (`tools/analysis/gradeFit.ts`, `npx tsx tools/analysis/fit-grades-cli.ts <manifest.json>` or `--cipi <index.json>`). Each item's label is mapped onto the points, its lowest value 10 and its highest 98, linear between (Henle 2 is 21, the first point of Intermediate; AX-3 provisional); each item is scored by the Rules model, a work by its hardest movement; `tail` is fitted by least squares over the items scored at or past `beginnerEnd`. The command prints the suggested `tail`, its mean error in points, the share landing in the right grade, the rank correlation of score and label, and how many items graded above Beginner score as Beginner (the Rules model orders those too low, and no `tail` can mend it). It writes nothing: a person accepts the number in `DIFFICULTY_WEIGHTS`. The game's points above Beginner are `upperPoints` (`src/core/piece/difficulty.ts`).

## Acceptance criteria

Slice A (built):

- **core_08.AC1** `difficultyOf` scores every bar from the notes the student plays, with the features of rule 2, as a pure tested function; four quarter notes of one pitch score far below mixed triplets that jump; accompaniment, tied-on notes and empty bars add nothing.
- **core_08.AC2** The piece's half stars (1 to 50) and points never fall when its score rises, and below `beginnerEnd` the half stars are the steps 1 to 10 of d138; on the real pieces Puuvaim's three phrases keep their order, every confirmed number is within one step of the proposal, and a one-hand tune sits below the two-hand pieces (`tests/content/pieceDifficulty.test.ts`).
- **core_08.AC3** `piece:lint` and the piece preview show the proposal, the confirmed number when there is one, and the hardest bars as printed.
- **core_08.AC4** A confirmed `difficulty` 2 or more half stars from the proposal is a `difficulty` warning, never an error.
- **core_08.AC5** The info card shows the grade of the piece's points and its five stars filled partly, four points a star, a grade's top its own (d157).
- **core_08.AC6** `points` runs 1 to 100, never falls when the score rises, and `scale` is `points` / 2 rounded up; a confirmed number is two points per half star.
- **core_08.AC7** Any list of bar ranges is scored as one passage in that order, the seams included and the written bar numbers kept; each named part gets its own score, and the piece preview shows them.
- **core_08.AC8** A bar list typed with printed numbers (`1-4, 9-12`, a pick-up as 0) is read into ranges in that order, refused with a reason when it cannot be, and written back the same way.
- **core_08.AC9** The parts of every bar's and piece's score, one per dimension, add up to its score.
- **core_08.AC10** The piece preview's difficulty panel shows the heat of every bar on the staff and in a strip, scores the passage clicked, shift-clicked or typed, and lists its dimensions and each bar's features.
- **core_08.AC11** A labelled set (our manifest, or CIPI's index) trains a learned model that is cross-validated with composers held out and written to `private/models/` unless the set is publishable; the game reads a model only if it was fitted on today's features, and the preview shows its level, marked private; scores it cannot read are listed with why.
- **core_08.AC12** A graded set (our manifest, or CIPI's index) is scored item by item, a work by its hardest movement; its labels map onto the points from 10 to 98; the fitted `tail` recovers the one a synthetic set was made with; the report gives the suggestion, its error, the share in the right grade, the rank correlation and the items scored too low, and nothing is written.
- **core_08.AC13** `measuredDifficulty` gives each bar its share of notes right first time over its latest plays and its gap from the piece's mean, falls back to the prior for a bar with too few plays and for a piece with fewer than two bars measured, and names at most three hardest bars for the player, in bar order.
- **core_08.AC14** The calibration tool turns a practice log into one sample per measured bar, its features centred on its piece, lists pieces it cannot find, refuses a fit on too few bars or an export without the practice log, finds the weight that made a synthetic set, and keeps the summed bar score unchanged.

## References

What the published work measures, for the calibration of slice B; the features of rule 2 are a subset of these.

- Ramoneda et al., [Combining piano performance dimensions for score difficulty classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08480) (CIPI, 652 pieces, Henle's levels 1 to 9) and [Towards explainable musical difficulty estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00473) (interpretable descriptors: pitch, rhythm, hand movement).
- Ramoneda et al., [Score difficulty analysis based on fingering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13010) (fingering features beat note-only features); Nakamura et al., [Statistical piano reduction controlling performance difficulty](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05006) (difficulty from playing rate and how rare a fingering is).
- [Predicting performance difficulty from piano sheet music images](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16287) (larger labelled sets: 9 and 5 levels).
- Holder et al., [Musiplectics](https://people.cs.vt.edu/~tilevich/papers/musiplectics.pdf) (a base cost per note and interval, multiplied by key, dynamics, articulation and length).
- Syncopation for a later rhythm feature: the Longuet-Higgins and Lee measure over the metrical hierarchy, tested by [Smith and Honing 2006](https://www.mcg.uva.nl/mcg-2023/papers/smith-honing-2006.pdf).
