# ft_1_02d: Editor extras

Part of ft_1_02 (item 7). An author writes dotted notes, enters notes from the EZ-220 or the keyboard, gets automatic note lengths, sets finger numbers and a BPM, all in the piece maker (`src/game/EditorScene.ts`, pure model in `src/core/editor/`, ft_1_02a). Index: [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md).

## Rules

1. **Dotted notes.** `LENGTHS` has the dotted half (3 quarters, `6` in the ABC; button "Dot half", key 3). The staff draws a half note with a dot. Dotted quarter and dotted eighth, and dotted rests, are not built.
2. **Automatic length (d078).** Every note, the first in a bar too, gets the selected length and is only shortened to the longest length that fits before the next note or the barline (`autoLength`). A note never crosses a barline or runs into the next note.
3. **Wheel.** With no note under the pointer the wheel cycles the length through `WHEEL_LENGTHS` (whole, half, quarter, eighth; `cycleLength`), stopping at the ends (in 3/4 at the half). Over a note the wheel moves it up or down and Ctrl + wheel changes its length (it was the other way round). Number keys and the tablet length buttons still work.
4. **Fingers.** An optional `finger` (1 to 5) on a note, set with the F key (none, 1 to 5, none) or the 1 to 5 buttons and AUTO on the copy row. The ABC uses `!1!` to `!5!` before the note (inside the brackets for a chord head); no schema bump (additive, d069). The staff draws it right of the head, past its ledger line or dot (`fingerX`); a chord shows the top head's finger unless every head's is on (`headFinger`). AUTO is the pure `autoFingers` (`src/core/editor/fingers.ts`), the fingering search of [ft_1_30](ft_1_30-auto-fingering.md).
5. **Finger numbers on the practice screen.** The gear screen has one three-state switch, Finger numbers OFF (default), TOP or ALL, saved per player (`fingerNumbers`, `showFingers`, `chordFingers`). Showing them counts as a hint and reduces XP (`hintDiscount`). The old monster-count row is gone; every battle is one monster.
6. **BPM.** One source: `chart.tempoMap[0].bpm`, from the ABC `Q:1/4=N`. An editor piece has an optional `bpm` (30 to 500, absent means 80), set with a field and minus and plus buttons on the second options row (`bpmOf`, `setBpm`, `stepBpm`). `tempoLabel` (`src/core/chart/tempoLabel.ts`) shows it on the menu info card and the Practice tools strip, in Wait mode too; in Tempo mode at another percent it also shows the effective BPM. Out of scope: metronome hotkey, tempo changes inside a piece.
7. **Piano entry.** A key on any keyboard except the computer keyboard writes a note at the cursor (AC9 to AC12, AC14). Core: `playEntry.ts`, `entryMachine.ts`, `entryPrefs.ts` (short-tap setting, per-device record, schemaVersion 1, `AX-3 provisional`, apart from the player save). The scene puts played entries into history once every key is up (a chord is one step). Recording in tempo is not built.
8. **Third tool row (d106).** Copy the bar, paste (drop-down x1, x2, x4) and ties (drop-down tie, pick, all; core_09.AC13). The drop-down is `PixelScene.openMenu` (`src/core/layout/dropMenu.ts`). A new piece starts on a start screen for meter and key; coming back from Settings keeps the piece.
9. **Bar editing.** Shift + left click selects bars; a left drag over 4 art pixels is a lasso (`barsInRect`); a band shows them (`barBoxes`). Ctrl+C, X, V copy, cut, paste: a cut moves later bars back, never below 4 bars; a paste right after a copy or paste goes after the selection, over another selection it replaces, with none it goes before the last clicked bar (`pasteAt`). Insert adds empty bars (`insertBlankBars`); Up and Down move the selection's notes (Ctrl: an octave); Delete clears; repeat signs move with inserted and removed bars (`src/core/editor/bars.ts`). A grip at the right edge of the selection drags out copies of it (`repeatHandle.ts`, AC24).
10. **Mouse buttons (`src/core/editor/mouse.ts`).** Middle button places notes and rests and lengthens a note (Ctrl shortens); Alt + left click does the same on a touchpad; left selects a note or moves the entry cursor; right removes. The canvas blocks Chrome's middle-button autoscroll.
11. **Keyboard-only writing.** Cursor moves are pure in `keyCursor.ts` (`timeStops`, `moveInTime`, `moveBar`, `movePitch`, `otherStaff`), edits in `keyEdits.ts` (`repeatTied`, `chordKeyPress` with `CHORD_SHAPES`, `moveNoteKeepingLegato`); the scene (`cursorKey`) only routes keys and draws the cursor. `AX-3 provisional:` the chord key is C, Enter leaves the cursor on the new note (Ctrl + Enter moves on), and the chord cycle goes on to the root alone after the seventh chord.
12. **Shortcut table.** Plain data in `src/game/editor/shortcuts.ts` (`EDITOR_KEYS`, `editorKeyOf`, `digitOf`); `PixelScene.markKey` names a key on its button while F1 is on. `AX-3 provisional:` the letters other than N and the digits: P listen, Y try, I the piece, R rests, A accidentals, T tie, L slur, D dynamics, F finger, K repeats, M staff, U short tap, V paste, C chord, and Ctrl + S, Z, Y, C, E, O.
13. Verified on the virtual device only (unit tests plus `tests/e2e/editor.spec.ts`, `tests/e2e/settings.spec.ts`); the practice-screen finger display has no e2e test yet.

## Acceptance criteria

- **ft_1_02d.AC1** A dotted half in 3/4 is drawn, compiled, judged and written by the editor; a piece entered from the piano equals the same piece clicked in.
- **ft_1_02d.AC2** A note added in the editor has the selected length; where that does not fit before the next note or the bar line it gets the longest length that does (a whole after a quarter in 4/4 becomes a dotted half); property test: any sequence of adds keeps every bar exactly full (d078).
- **ft_1_02d.AC3** The wheel with no note under the pointer cycles the current length in both directions and stops at the ends.
- **ft_1_02d.AC4** A finger set in the editor survives save, load and the ABC round trip, and is drawn on the staff beside its note (right of the head).
- **ft_1_02d.AC5** An own piece has a BPM (30 to 500, 80 when absent) that is set in the editor and survives the ABC (`Q:`), the compiled chart's tempo map and a piece file; an old piece or file without one loads unchanged and plays at 80, with no file version bump.
- **ft_1_02d.AC7** The wheel over a note moves it one staff position up (wheel up) or down (wheel down): the next letter, with the octave at B and C. The key signature decides the sharp or flat, so the note keeps its key. Stepping past the keyboard range is refused.
- **ft_1_02d.AC8** The wheel over a note, or anywhere in the column of a chord on its staff, moves every note of the chord one staff position: all of them or none (a step past the keyboard range is refused for the whole chord). Ctrl + wheel there changes the chord's length, as over a note. Ctrl + wheel on a placed rest changes the rest's length the same way, through the plain rest lengths (sixteenth to whole; no dotted rests are placed), and the plain wheel there still cycles the current length.
- **ft_1_02d.AC6** The BPM of a piece is shown on the info card and in Practice in Wait mode too, from one pure helper (`tempoLabel`); in Tempo mode at another percent it also shows the effective BPM.
- **ft_1_02d.AC13** AUTO fingers: fills the finger of every note that has none with the cheapest fingering of its hand (ft_1_30), the hand position of the player's level (`fingerings` in `content/curriculum/levels.json`) preferred; a finger the author set is kept; a note no hand can finger (a chord of more than five keys) is left and counted. Pure function `autoFingers`.
- **ft_1_02d.AC9** Playing a key on any keyboard (EZ-220, on-screen; not the computer keyboard since ft_1_02d.AC14) writes a note at the cursor of its staff: in Auto a key from middle C (C4) up goes on the right-hand staff, a key below it on the left-hand staff; the staff button fixes it to one staff. Each staff has its own cursor.
- **ft_1_02d.AC10** Hold length: under 1 s a quarter, 1 to 2 s a half, 2 to 3 s a dotted half, 3 s or more a whole; a short tap (under 150 ms) is an eighth only with the short-tap toggle on (off by default); keys pressed within 80 ms of each other are one chord.
- **ft_1_02d.AC11** Backspace removes the last entry written by playing (a note, a chord or a rest); one entry is one undo step (Ctrl+Z takes back exactly that entry).
- **ft_1_02d.AC12** Rests are written by playing: with the Notes/Rests switch on Rests a played key writes a rest on its staff (held spacebar is a rest too, for as long as it is held).
- **ft_1_02d.AC14** In the piece maker the computer keyboard is not a piano (it is for testing): its letters, digits and arrows are the editor's keys and write no note; the EZ-220 and the on-screen piano still do.
- **ft_1_02d.AC15** A keyboard cursor (a place on one staff and a pitch, drawn as a line across its staff and a ring at its pitch): the first arrow shows it where writing goes on; left and right go to the previous or next note, chord or rest start of its hand, in free time by the selected length, across bar lines; Ctrl + left or right to a bar start; up and down one staff step, so it stops on each head of a chord; Ctrl + up or down jumps between the spaces next to each staff's outer lines, A2, G3, F4, E5 and round (never onto a ledger line), changing staff at the same time. On a note it selects that note, so the tools (F, the lengths, Delete) act on it; on a free place the next played key goes there. A left click puts it where it clicked.
- **ft_1_02d.AC16** Shift + up or down moves the note under the cursor (one head, not its chord) a staff step, Ctrl + Shift an octave, and the cursor goes with it; with bars selected it moves their notes. A tie into or out of that note that no longer joins one pitch becomes a slur over the same two notes, and back to a tie when the pitch returns; the middle note of three tied notes gets one slur over all three; where a slur cannot be drawn (it would overlap another, or cross a repeat sign) the tie stays stored and simply is not drawn. Shift + left or right makes the note (its chord) shorter or longer. Ctrl + Shift + left or right selects bars from the cursor's bar.
- **ft_1_02d.AC17** Enter writes a note (a rest in rest mode) of the selected length at the cursor, and the cursor stays on it, with it selected; Ctrl + Enter writes it and moves the cursor on to the next place. Shift + Enter writes the note or chord under the cursor again in the free place right after it, tied to it, and the cursor goes to the new note; refused with a reason when that place is taken.
- **ft_1_02d.AC18** C builds a chord on the cursor's pitch as its root, in the key of the piece (D in C major: D F A); pressed again it cycles 1-3-5, 1-5, 1-3, 1-3-5-7, 1, and back. The shape is read from the notes at the root's place, by letter (so undo and redo need no memory); a press takes away only the steps the next shape lacks and adds the missing ones, so the root and the notes that stay keep their length, accidental, finger, tie and slur; notes of other steps stay.
- **ft_1_02d.AC19** Every tool of the piece maker has a key, matched by physical key so it works on any layout: F1 shows each key on its button (`^` is Ctrl) and the help pictures over the staff ([ft_1_33](ft_1_33-piece-maker-picture-help.md): the mouse, and Kiur's tour of the cursor keys), F1 or Esc hides them. A key with choices opens its numbered list: N the note lengths (1 whole, 3 dotted half, 2 half, 4 quarter, 8 eighth), R the same for rests, T the ties and slurs, D, F (1 to 5, 6 Auto, 0 none), M the staff, V the paste kinds; A (sharp, flat, natural, in turn) and K (the repeat's bars) act at once as their item does (one item each); a digit picks a choice, Esc closes the list unchanged, and any other key (Enter, an arrow) takes the list's default and then does its own work. The pre-note has no key.
- **ft_1_02d.AC20** After a note or rest is written (a click or Enter), the length of the next one is guessed from it (`src/core/editor/nextLength.ts`): a note that ends inside a beat is followed by what completes the beat (after one eighth another, after a dotted quarter an eighth, after a dotted eighth a sixteenth); back on a beat after shorter notes, the quarter again (two eighths, then a quarter); after a quarter or longer, the same length; a length the rest of the bar cannot hold gives way to the longest it can. The Length item shows the guess, and a parent's own pick overrides it for the next note. AX-3 provisional: the beat is the quarter in every meter.
- **ft_1_02d.AC21** The time panel's time steps 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 and A (Auto) (`src/core/editor/meterGuess.ts`): the piece is written in 4/4 and no length is guessed until the first bar sets the time. When the parent leaves the first bar (a click or the cursor in a later bar) or fills it, a panel over the staff's foot asks: 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4 with the first bar's length lit (whole beats, a part beat counts as one; those the bar does not fit in greyed) and a tick that takes it; a press on a time takes that one (a pickup bar is the case to correct). Enter takes the lit one; while the panel is open no other key writes; Esc closes it and it asks again on the next move out of the first bar. The click that asked writes nothing. AX-3 provisional: only the three quarter-note times; the confirmed time is an undoable edit.
- **ft_1_02d.AC22** Enter on a note the cursor is on makes it a step longer (as a middle click does); Alt + Enter makes it as long as the room to the next note or the bar line allows, and on a free place writes the longest note that fits there (`longestFit` in `src/core/editor/model.ts`). Ctrl + Enter on a note still moves on.
- **ft_1_02d.AC23** AUTO fingers by bar: the fingers AUTO wrote are marked as its own (`fingerAuto`, an optional field, so a piece from before reads as all its fingers the author's). When the author sets or clears a finger, AUTO's own fingers in that bar of that hand are fitted again around it in the same undo step, the fingers outside the bar held as they stand; the author's fingers and the notes with no finger never change. AUTO on the whole piece fills the notes with none and fits its own again. The mark lives in the own piece and its piece file only; after an ABC or chart round trip AUTO's fingers read as the author's (safe: nothing is overwritten). A tie across the bar line is not followed: the held head in the bar before keeps its finger. `AX-3 provisional:` always on, no switch. TODO(architect, R2): ask the maintainer after a week of use whether a switch is wanted.
- **ft_1_02d.AC24** Drag-repeat: a grip at the right edge of the bar selection's last bar (`repeatHandleBox`). Dragged right, it shows ghost bars and the count of copies, in whole selection lengths rounded up (`repeatCopies`; past the piece's last bar it counts on by that bar's width); let go, the copies go in right after the selection with paste's rules (`insertBars`: empty bars at the end used up first, `MAX_BARS` refused with a reason), one undo step. Dragged back onto the selection or before it, nothing goes in.
