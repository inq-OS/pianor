# ft_1_36: Songs made of pieces

Index: [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md).

A long song is written and learned a part at a time. An 82-bar song may have only its intro (bars 1 to 12) and its outro (bars 71 to 82) written; the 58 bars between are planned and filled in later. AX-0: the student reads and plays every part on its own and then the song as a whole, which is how a real piece is learned; AX-1: the whole song is the harder pass and the one that earns the song's stars.

## One model for parts and songs

A piece's named parts ([core_06](core_06-piece-decomposition.md), the `phrases` of its meta file) and a song's parts are one tree, shown on one screen. A song's tree holds pieces; a piece's tree holds its phrases (bar ranges); a piece in a song unfolds into its phrases as one more level. Phrases stay where they are (the meta file, read by the generators, the linter and the characters), so nothing that uses them changes. The song page (`SongScene`) takes the place of the part cards (`PartsScene`): a piece with parts and no song opens it with the piece at the root.

## Rules

1. **The tree.** A song is a file of nodes, each with an id that never changes (saves and links point at it). Four kinds:
   - **group**: a name and nodes inside it, like a folder (Verse 1 holding its two halves);
   - **piece**: one piece of the game (its own ABC and meta, or an own piece), named by its title unless the node gives a name;
   - **link**: the same piece again where the song repeats it (a chorus sung three times is written once and linked twice, like a symlink); it points at a piece node earlier in the same song and has its own name and its own on and off; the stars and the learning are the piece's, so they are shared;
   - **blank**: a part not written yet, with a name and a planned number of bars.
   Any part may be a group or a plain piece (an intro may be a folder of pieces or one piece). A group plays its rows joined in order, so it is exactly the sum of the parts under it; a piece plays its own notes, and the rows under a piece are its named bar ranges (core_06), which may nest. A piece belongs to one song at most. `AX-3 provisional:` at most four levels of groups under the song; a link points at a piece, never at a group.
2. **Song bars.** Bars are counted through the whole song, blanks and parts that are off included, so the outro of the example is bars 71 to 82 wherever it is shown. A blank counts its planned bars until it is written; then the written piece's bars count.
3. **Defaults pass down.** The song sets a tempo (BPM), a key (major or minor) and a meter. A group or a piece may set its own value of any of them; every node without one takes its parent's. Where a node sets its own value it shows that value beside the parent's, which is crossed out: `♩ ~~90~~ 70`, `~~C~~ G`, `~~4/4~~ 3/4`. A node that follows shows the value alone, in plain ink. The value a node set can be cleared, and it follows again.
4. **A piece always holds its own notes and numbers.** A piece is a full piece on its own (its ABC `Q:`, `K:` and `M:` are written out), so it plays, is learned and is counted the same inside a song and alone. Changing the song's tempo writes the new tempo into every piece that follows it. Changing the song's key transposes the notes of every piece that follows it by the same interval, spelled in the new key; a piece with its own key stays. When a new key is picked, the rows that would change show their new key faintly and the transposed first bar of the first such piece is drawn beside the circle (d286); nothing changes until Apply is pressed, which names what it changes ("Apply: 3 pieces"); the whole change is one Undo step. A transposed piece keeps following, so a later key change moves it again from where it is. The preview warns of every note the transposition puts outside the piece's level, with the piece and bar named, and Apply still takes it (a warning never blocks, as in the piece maker); only a note outside the keyboard (C2 to C7) refuses the change, named the same way. A meter is never rewritten: a changed song meter applies to blanks and new pieces, and a piece written in another meter shows its own as set.
5. **On and off, per player.** Each row of the tree has a switch. A part that is off is left out of the whole song and of its group; a group that is off turns everything inside it off. At least one written part stays on ("One part must stay on."). Saved per player and song by node id, like pieces on and off ([ft_1_02c](ft_1_02c-pieces-on-and-off.md)); a node id that no longer exists is ignored. Turning a part off changes no stars, XP or learning. `AX-3 provisional:` the choice is each player's, as with pieces.
6. **Playing.** Each row that can be played has its own play button, a large triangle at the row's right end; a press on it plays the row: the song's own row (the first) the whole song, a group the group, a piece or a link that piece alone, exactly as from the piece list (its own stars, XP and learning), a phrase the phrase as a section (core_06). A press on the rest of a row only selects it (lit), so a child who opens the screen to look or to turn parts off never starts play by mistake. Blanks and parts that are off have no play button.
7. **The whole song on one staff.** A song or group plays as one chart joined from its written parts that are on, in order (`songChart`, pure): the tempo, key and meter change where a part sets its own, drawn as on any score (a tempo mark over the bar, a key signature, a time signature). Each part is a phrase of the joined chart, so a part's name stands over its first bar as a labelled bracket, and sections ([core_03](core_03-section-repeat-and-tempo-percent.md)) may cross from one part into the next. Where a blank or a part that is off was left out, a thin fold in the staff carries its name and bars ("Verse 1, bars 13 to 36, not written"). The tempo percent applies to the whole tempo map; Listen plays the song through.
8. **Paging through the song.** The staff pages as every piece does (`<` and `>`, the arrow keys, the wheel). Over the paper runs the **song map**: one block per part in song order, as wide as its bars but never narrower than a finger (`AX-3 provisional:` 16 cells, the long parts give way), its name in it when it fits, blanks hatched, parts that are off pale, a link with the curled arrow of its row; the page on screen is a white bar under the blocks (`AX-3 provisional:` a bar, not a frame, so it never covers a name), and the part being played is a lit block, so the two never look alike. A press on a block turns the staff to that part's first page and flashes its name bracket; it never starts play there (a tap on a bar does, core_03 rule 8). The map is shown for a song and a group, never for a lone piece.
9. **Records.** The whole song is its own entry in the records (`song:<id>`): its passes, stars and development graph, like a piece. A whole-song pass earns XP by the usual rule for its notes; a group earns none (a section). A whole-song pass changes no piece's record. `AX-3 provisional:` the whole song earns its stars only with every written part on; a pass with a part off is scored and shown but not kept as a best.
10. **The song page** (`SongScene`) looks like the piece book: the same open book, rows of the same height, the same Previous, page number and Next at its foot, the rows running from the left page on to the right. The first row is the whole song: title, song bars, tempo, key, meter, best stars and its buttons. Under it the tree as an outline: each row a node, numbered by its place (1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.2.1) and indented by its depth, with any mix of groups and pieces at any depth. A row shows the switch (a box, ticked while the part is in the song; a player's first visit is told once that a tick puts a part in the song), the triangle when it has rows under it, its number and name, the song bars, tempo, key and meter (rule 3), the best stars, and the book's buttons (play, the piece's stats `S`, `i`); a link's and a blank's name carry a small mark of their kind (a curled arrow, a dashed outline). An own value stands in red ink on a small chip, with its sign (♩, the key's name, the meter), beside the crossed-out value it replaces, so a different value is seen at a glance; a follower's value is in pale ink. A press on a value, chip or not, opens its choices as slot 2, 3 or 4 of the bubble does. The triangle folds and unfolds the rows under it and answers over the row's whole height; a folded row shows how many rows it hides; a piece with phrases unfolds into them. A row, a switch, the triangle and a button are each at least a finger wide (`AX-3 provisional:` 11 cells, as the piece book's own small buttons), with a gap between switch and triangle. A part that is off is pale over its whole row and its box empty. The switch of the last written part that is on is drawn faded and says why when pressed ("One part must stay on."). Keyboard: the first key only shows the selection, so no key plays by surprise; Up and Down walk the rows (and turn the page at its end), Page Up and Page Down turn the page, Right and Left unfold and fold, Space turns the switch, Enter plays; a key that does nothing on the rows (Up on the first, Left on a row that does not fold) moves the focus ring to the strip, the pager and the corners; Shift + F10 or the context-menu key opens the bubble (ft_1_20). The strip at the foot: Undo and Redo, More at the left (ft_1_20), Play at the right end, naming what it plays ("Play Chorus"; the whole song when no row is selected). Back leads to the songs list.
11. **Blanks are written from the song.** A blank's row has a Write button (a pen) in place of the play button. It opens the piece maker ([ft_1_02a](ft_1_02a-editor-model-and-screen.md)) with a new own piece of the blank's bars, all empty, in the tempo, key and meter the blank takes, titled with the song and the blank's name. Saving it turns the blank into a piece node with the same id, so its switch and any link keep working. A press on the rest of the row only selects it, as on any row.
12. **Making a song.** The songs list's first row "+ New song" (the piece book's own first card stays "+ New piece"). A new song asks its title and starts with its defaults (4/4, C major, 80 BPM, `AX-3 provisional`) and one blank. Editing is in the song page through the bubble ([ft_1_20](ft_1_20-action-and-inventory-strip.md): a right click, a long press, two fingers, the context-menu key, or More), in fixed slots. On a row: 0 add inside or after (a group, a blank, a new piece, a piece of the player's own, a link to an earlier piece); 1 name and labels (rule 16); 2 tempo; 3 key (the circle of fifths, [ft_1_28](ft_1_28-circle-of-fifths.md)); 4 meter; 5 write (a blank) or edit (a piece) in the piece maker; 6 move (up, down, into the group above, out of its group, each shown as a faint line where the row will land before it is picked, d286); 7 remove from the song (a bin, never the cross of Close; no question: Undo is in the strip and Kiur says "Removed Chorus."). Tempo, key and meter each offer **Follow the song** (or the group) beside their values, which clears an own value. An option's choices open in a row under the selected row, never over other rows. On empty paper: add at the end. A row may also be dragged by its picture; a line shows where it will land before it is let go. Removing a piece node removes it from the song only; the piece stays in the player's pieces. Every edit undoes (Undo, Ctrl+Z). Bundled songs are read only in the game (no edit slots); a person writes their tree in the song file.
13. **The songs list.** The piece book has a song bookmark, with its own picture and apart from the book's other bookmarks (`AX-3 provisional:` at the strip's right end, away from the filter; the songs list has the pieces' bookmark in the same place); a press on it opens the songs list in the same book, one row a song with what to know before opening it: title, best stars of the whole song, bars written of bars planned ("24 of 82 bars"), its parts, tempo and key, and the book's buttons (play the whole song, its stats `S`, `i`). A press on a song's title opens its song page (rule 10). The first row is "+ New song" (rule 12). The pieces of a song are not listed on their own in the piece book or in the library's drawers; the song is filed there by its title and turned on and off as one.
14. **Files.** A bundled song is `content/songs/<id>/song.json` (zod schema, `schemaVersion` 1, `title`, `titles`, `source`, `license`, `status`, the defaults and the tree), its pieces in `content/pieces/` as now. `piece:lint` also checks every song: each piece exists and is in no other song, each link points at an earlier piece node, node ids are unique, a following piece's tempo and key equal the song's, and every piece passes at its level. An own song is kept with the own pieces (browser storage, licence `NaN`, never committed) and exported and imported as one file holding the song and its pieces ([ft_1_02b](ft_1_02b-own-pieces-as-files.md)).
15. **Learning a song.** A song is learned piece by piece in song order, each by its own plan ([ft_1_32](ft_1_32-learn-a-piece-bar-by-bar.md)), a link only once; then the whole song, three plays as a whole piece is learned ([d300](../decisions/game.md#d300)): the first two go on past a mistake, the third perfect. A character's song is such a song, its pieces the character's levels (4 to 16 bars) and the whole song its final tug (ft_1_32 slice 4).
16. **Labels.** Any part may carry labels from a ready-made list (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, solo, outro, etude, practice; a labels file beside the songs, each with a string key so it has its Estonian name) and words of the author's own. A new part takes the name of its first label until it is named ("Chorus"). A row shows its labels as small tabs after its name. Each label of the song has a switch in the song page's strip (`AX-3 provisional:` the strip is the page's header), lit while every part with it is on; a press turns every part with that label on or off at once (all choruses), with the switch rules of rule 5. `piece:lint` warns of a label word not on the list in a bundled song. `AX-3 provisional:` the list above; a word used by many songs joins it.
17. **Writing a piece into a song.** A song page's row offers New piece (slot 0) and its piece maker opens with the song's or group's tempo, key and meter. Saving a new piece, from the song or from the piece book's "+ New piece", asks once where it goes: on its own in the piece book (the default from the piece book) or into an own song, at a place in its tree picked as rule 12's move (a faint line shows where), with a new group made on the way when asked. The piece is saved once, as one own piece; a song only points at it, so the same piece is never saved twice and never stands in two songs.
18. **Moving a piece into a song.** An own piece standing on its own is moved into an own song from the song page (slot 0, a piece of the player's own) or from the piece book (the piece's bubble, Move to a song), placed as in rule 17. Once in a song it leaves the piece book's list (rule 13); removed from the song, it stands on its own in the piece book again, with its stars, XP and learning untouched. A bundled piece is never moved.

## Acceptance criteria

Slice 1:

- **ft_1_36.AC1** The schema takes groups, pieces, links and blanks with unique ids; it refuses a link to a later node or to a group, a piece in two songs, and more than four levels of groups, each with the node named.
- **ft_1_36.AC2** Every node's tempo, key and meter is its own value or its parent's, and the resolved tree says which (property test over random trees).
- **ft_1_36.AC3** Song bars count blanks at their planned bars and parts that are off; the example song (intro 12 bars, blank 58, outro 12) puts the outro at bars 71 to 82.
- **ft_1_36.AC4** `songChart` joins the written parts that are on in order, links repeating their piece's notes; its notes equal those of the pieces one after another; a part with its own tempo, key or meter adds a change to the tempo, key or meter map at its first bar; every part is a phrase of the chart, nested as in the tree.
- **ft_1_36.AC5** `piece:lint` reports a missing piece, a piece in two songs, a bad link, a duplicate id and a following piece whose tempo or key differs from the song's, each with the song and node named.

Slice 2:

- **ft_1_36.AC6** The piece book's song bookmark opens the songs list in the same book: per song its title, best stars, bars written of planned, parts, tempo and key; its play button plays the whole song and a press on its title opens the song page; its pieces are not listed on their own in the piece book nor in the library's drawers, where the song is one card that turns it on and off as one (a song that is off leaves the songs list); its `i` button opens the song's info card.
- **ft_1_36.AC7** The song page looks like the piece book and pages like it; it shows the whole song first, then the tree as a numbered outline (1, 1.1, 1.2.1) of groups and pieces in any mix, with each row's kind, name, song bars, tempo, key and meter, an own value on a red chip beside the crossed-out default; groups and pieces with phrases fold and unfold; the rows are walked, folded, switched and played by the keyboard as rule 10 says.
- **ft_1_36.AC8** A row's play button plays it: the song or a group as one staff, a piece alone with its own record, a phrase as a section; a press elsewhere on a row only selects it; a blank or a part that is off has no play button.
- **ft_1_36.AC9** The whole song shows each part's name over its first bar, the tempo, key and meter changes where a part sets its own, and a fold with name and bars where a blank or a part that is off was left out; it pages to the end and a section may cross two parts.
- **ft_1_36.AC10** The song map shows every part as wide as its bars, never narrower than its minimum, blanks hatched and parts that are off pale, the page on screen marked and the part being played lit; a tap on a block turns to that part without starting play.
- **ft_1_36.AC11** A switch turns a part or group off for this player only and survives a reload; the last written part on cannot be turned off and its switch is faded; a player's first song page says once, on the first part's row, what a tick does; stars, XP and learning are untouched.
- **ft_1_36.AC12** A whole-song pass is kept under the song with its stars and graph and earns XP; a group pass earns none; neither changes a piece's record.
- **ft_1_36.AC20** A part's labels come from the ready-made list or the author's own words and show as tabs on its row; a new part is named by its first label; a press on a label in the page's header turns every part with it on or off, keeping the last written part on (rule 16).
- **ft_1_36.AC13** A piece with parts and no song opens the song page with its phrases as rows, and behaves as core_06.AC4 and AC8 ask.

Slice 3:

- **ft_1_36.AC14** "+ New" makes a song from its title with one blank; rows are added, renamed, moved (a line previews where), and removed through the bubble's fixed slots and by dragging, by mouse, long press and keyboard; Follow the song clears an own value; every edit undoes.
- **ft_1_36.AC15** A blank's Write button opens the piece maker with that many empty bars in the tempo, key and meter it takes; the saved piece takes the blank's place and id.
- **ft_1_36.AC16** Setting the song's tempo writes it into every piece that follows; setting the key transposes every piece that follows, shown faintly and taken only on Apply, warns with piece and bar of a note that leaves the piece's level and is refused only when a note would leave C2 to C7; a piece with its own value is untouched; clearing an own value makes the node follow.
- **ft_1_36.AC17** An own song exported and imported gives the same song and pieces, as new own pieces with free ids.
- **ft_1_36.AC21** Saving a new piece asks once where it goes, on its own or at a picked place in an own song (a new group made on the way); it is saved as one own piece whichever piece maker saved it; an own piece moved into a song leaves the piece book and returns to it when removed from the song, its record untouched.
- **ft_1_36.AC18** Is the song page clear to a parent writing a song and to the student choosing parts? [human]

Slice 4:

- **ft_1_36.AC19** A song is learned piece by piece in song order, a linked piece once, then the whole song three plays as a whole piece is (d300); a character's song gives one level per piece and its whole song is the final tug.
