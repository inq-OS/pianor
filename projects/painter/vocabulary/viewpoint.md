# Viewpoint and perspective

Where the 1990s games put the eye, and how they bend a town round it. Words only; the pictures are in the catalogue.

## The eye stands above the ground (mi1-town-from-above, mi2-wharf-concept, mi3-town-terrace)
- None of the three is seen from a walker's eye. The town at night is seen from above its roofs, the wharf from the top of a ramp a storey up, the later game's town from a walled terrace over the water.
- The horizon sits in the upper third of the picture or is pushed out of it altogether; from two thirds to the whole of the picture is ground, roofs and water seen from above.
- Because the eye is high, the ground is a shape and not a strip: a street that bends in an S, a quay that curls to the sea, a ramp of steps. The way a walker will take can be read like a map.
- Roofs are planes with an area, as big as the walls under them or bigger. A house is read by its roof first.
- The far place the walker goes to (a jetty, a second town) is small, low and far, and the path to it is drawn whole.
- What the eye stands on is in the picture, dark and cut by the bottom edge: a parapet with balls on its posts, a near roof, a lamp post, a bollard. It tells where the viewer is and frames the bright middle.

## Houses fan out and bend over the street (mi1-town-street-night, mi1-town-from-above, mi2-wharf-concept, mi3-town-terrace)
- Upright edges are not upright. They fan out from a point below the picture, so every house is wider at its eave than at its foot and leans away from the picture's middle at the top; the further from the middle, the more it leans.
- On top of that each storey steps out over the one below and the roof overhangs like a hat, so the top of a house is wider again.
- Near houses run off the top edge. A house that fits whole into the picture is a far one.
- A row of houses follows a curve, never one straight line to one vanishing point: each house has its own, a little turned from its neighbour's, and each is plainly smaller than the one before.
- The walls themselves curve: a belly in the middle of a wall, a foot that spreads, a ridge that sags. The later game pushes this furthest (walls like sacks, roofs like caps).
- Sizes jump: something very big and near, something tiny and far, little in between.
- A street-level picture keeps the same tricks: the nearest gable takes a third of the picture, its roof twice as wide as its foot, and a small lit arch far off gives the depth.
