# Maps

## The island maps of the 1991 game (mi2-bg-0-002, mi2-bg-1-045, mi2-bg-2-026-mi2-scabb-map-owner)
- **The island fills the picture.** It is a fat mass taking about three quarters of the width and most of the height; the sea is a rim round it. The scroll with the name sits in the lower left over the sea, the compass rose in a free corner.
- **Places are small painted scenes, far larger than to scale** (the places of interest are deliberately oversized, the cemetery, the town and the bonfire, and the rocks too). A town is a handful of roofs and masts, a graveyard a fence and a few crosses, a fire one glow. Each sits on the coast or a height where it shows against the sea or a dark slope. Nothing else stands on the ground: no path lines, no dots, only a river.
- **Sea rocks are big:** each stack 4 to 8 per cent of the island's width, heaped lumps in three or four tones with dark seams, a pale ring of ripples round the foot and a darker one further out.
- **The land is dark, with relief brushed on:** broad strokes of dark and mid green that lie along the ridges, a ridge with one lit and one shaded flank. By day the land's median is a dark olive (about 0x334b1b), the lit strokes few; at night the land is nearly black and the sea is the brightest thing.
- **The island has thickness:** along the near coast a cliff face in vertical slabs of brown, with a dark line under it.
- **The sea:** one saturated blue with paint grain, a pale halo of shallows round the coast (by day almost white at the shore), thin dark wavy lines following the coast a few pixels out, in broken pieces. No white foam line.
- **Clouds** are soft and lie over the island as well as the sea.
- Ours (`mapPicture.ts`): the true outline from the orthophoto, a thin sliver from corner to corner that leaves two thirds of the picture to the sea; houses and the tower drawn larger than to scale already (`HOUSE_PLAN` 1.5, `TOWER_SCALE` 2.1) but still too small to point at; grass as fine noise in light greens (very dark pixels 3%, MI2 day 25 to 45%); a wide pale shingle belt; a white surf outline; boulders as dots; every path as a thin line. Gauge beside the MI2 day rooms: 18%.
- IDEA(painter): a caricature step between the world model and the map (wider across the island's short side, the empty tail shorter, the ground swelling round the places), kept as numbers in one place so every departure from the real island can be listed. Sketch 1 tried three strengths.
- Decided: option B, fat Keri (d125).

## Places, houses and rocks on our map
- **Oversized houses need room.** Drawn at twice their ground plan they sat on one another; they were spread out, each a named way (the wooden shed off the lighthouse, the boat shed toward the water, the well house east, the power station west). A map moves its places apart before it lets them overlap; the shore stays.
- **A small thing stays small.** The gull mummy's shed is much smaller than it seemed: oversizing is for places of interest, not for every roof.
- **Rocks are not sown evenly.** Ours were placed far too evenly; on the orthophoto the north-west end is thick with boulders, the north shore has a fair number, the south few. Clumps of big and small, some right by the shore.
- **Rocks come out of the water as leaning slabs** (the ellipses marked on the 1991 beach, mi2-bg-0-004): dark wedges with a slanted paler top plane and an edge running down from the peak, several leaning the same way in a clump, the water pale and broken round their feet. Our round lumps read as pebbles, and a string of them as a necklace.
- **A terrace may reach the water.** It may be drawn as if it reaches the waterfront: a map may stretch a small thing to tie a place to its shore.

## The lattice mast and the warden's house, from the reference photographs
- The mast is rust-brown steel all over, square, wide at the foot and narrowing, with a horizontal frame every few metres and crossed braces between; thin stays. From far off it reads as a dark red-brown ladder against the sky, taller than the house's ridge by three or four times. On the map: no trusses, only the taper and the frames.
- The house's sea face is flat: a long plastered wall, pale and stained, eight windows in a row, the door in the middle under a low gable that does not stand out of the wall, three chimneys on a grey tin roof. Before the door a low wooden deck with a rail and red benches; orange buoys hang on it. Pale round shingle lies between it and the water.
- IDEA(painter): the low gable over the door and the buoys on the terrace, when the house is next drawn large (the garden scene, the docks).


## Sea, ways and the coast
- Open sea is not empty on those maps: small wave strokes of two or three humps lie on it, sparse, and a place can be hidden in it for whoever looks long (the sand strip as a creature's back).
- Lines on the ground are for paved ways only; trodden paths are left off, since they only clutter the space.
- A map may sharpen a point of the coast and sink a corner of it, when the maintainer decides: the measured outline is where the map starts.
- The island sits as high and as large in the picture as its tallest thing allows, the name and the rose in what sea is left.

## Changing a map
- **Change only what was named, where it was named.** An extra fix is a change the reviewer sees: a rock moved to look less dropped reads as a dock moved with extra things added. Keep the rest where it was.
- **An oversized thing eats the ways that lead to it.** The house at twice its plan covered the start of its path, and the tower stood on its own track, so the ways looked cut off and one ran round the tower. A way on the map starts at the drawn thing's edge and runs to the drawn thing's foot; bushes keep off it.
- **A dock is a thing, not a line's end:** a road that runs straight into the water reads as wrong. A road stops at the shore and a pier of planks takes over, with a landing.
- **Distances between places are read against each other,** not against the scale.
- **A long house may be shortened** so that it does not compete with its surroundings: by a window's worth at each end, door in the middle.

## A village on an island map
- Houses on a map have character, never plain boxes, and a group of houses reads as a village: do not individualize each building, give a general picture of it.
- **The 1992 concept painting of the Phatt map** (marker and pencil, the artist's sheet): the town is some ten tiny houses heaped on a slope, each a crooked box no wider than a palm's crown, overlapping its neighbours, roofs in three or four loud colours (red, orange, blue) over pale or violet walls, a dot or two for windows, one pale path winding out of the heap. The whole town is a twentieth of the island's width. A lone house (the mansion, the cottage) gets one telling thing: a porch on posts, a stair, a red roof.
- **The 1997 map** (the circle marked on it): the town is one mass, cream walls and orange roofs run together along the bay, an arch and a tower sticking out of it; no house can be counted.
- What both do: **the group is the shape**, a house is a tile in it; roofs carry the colour, walls are a pale strip under them; nothing is square to anything else; the group sits in a dent of the green, with a darker edge of bushes behind it.
- IDEA(painter): for Keri's houses by the lighthouse, one heap of small crooked houses at the tower's foot, roofs in the photographs' colours (pale blue tin, red, bare boards), overlapping, smaller than now; the tower rises out of the heap.

## Houses with character

- **Character at the same sizes:** a house keeps its size and place; a sketch that moves a house loses to one that leaves it. Offer the look and the move as separate choices.
- **A house on our map is a crooked cottage:** a house lying east to west shows its long wall (leaning, dark panes between coloured shutters, a door, violet shade under the eaves and at the end away from the light) under a roof as high as the wall that hangs over it and sags along its ridge, in three flat bands with a glint on the ridge and a patch of rust; a house lying north to south shows its gable with the roof running back, both slopes seen from above. Colours are the photographs', louder. A green hut on a green meadow is painted sea green or it is lost. Code: `cottage`, `sideCottage`, `endCottage` in `mapPicture.ts`.
- **Painting a leaning wall without gaps:** ask every pixel what part of the house lies there (along the wall, rows over its foot) and make the lean a shift of whole screen rows. Putting pixels from the house's side leaves one-pixel holes, which the outline pass turns into black crosses.
- **The lighthouse gets character in its stone, never in its stance:** courses as short broken lines, light in flat bands, rusted hoops with rust run down under them. Wonky means wrong.
- **Do not misplace things:** a thing moved to solve a painting problem is a thing misplaced (the well clear of the fork, the memorial pushed by the tower's swell, the dock). Each place is held against the island as known. When two things collide on the map, ask the maintainer or let one pass behind the other; do not move either.
- **No path or staircase from the terrace to the waterfront:** "as if it reaches the waterfront" means the terrace itself may reach the shore, not a walk from it. A ring on a picture with no label: ask what it means before keeping or changing the thing.
